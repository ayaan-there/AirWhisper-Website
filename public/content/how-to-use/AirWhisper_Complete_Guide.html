AirWhisper: Complete System & User Operation Guide

Empowering non-verbal and motor-impaired individuals through touchless, gesture-driven wearable communication.

1. Clinical Context & Target Audience

Who AirWhisper Is Built For

AirWhisper is an assistive wearable communication system designed for individuals who have lost the ability to speak fluently or operate standard touchscreens due to severe motor impairments, neurological conditions, or physical trauma.

The reality for motor-impaired patients: isolation and frustration from standard touch interfaces.

Target Medical Conditions

  • Parkinson’s Disease: Severe resting and action tremors, bradykinesia (extreme slowness of movement), and limb rigidity that make touching small screen buttons virtually impossible.
  • Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS): Progressive loss of voluntary muscle control, speech dysarthria, and finger dexterity, while retaining gross arm or wrist mobility in early-to-mid stages.
  • Post-Stroke Conditions (Hemiplegia / Aphasia): Loss of functional use of one side of the body, fine motor coordination deficit, and expressive language difficulty.
  • Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI): Coordination disorders, tremors, ataxia, and cognitive-motor dissociation.
  • Road Traffic Accidents (RTA) & Spinal Cord Trauma: Quadriparesis or upper limb trauma where vocal cords or fine finger joints are immobilized.

The Global Crisis in Augmentative Communication (AAC)

  • 26.3 Million patients globally rely on Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) apps.
  • Severity Breakdown: 17.5% Mild, 43.8% Moderate, 38.7% Severe.
  • Why Traditional AAC Fails: Standard tablet AAC apps require pinpoint finger accuracy; for tremor patients, every attempt triggers accidental mis-hits and frustration. Eye-tracking rigs cost thousands of dollars, require rigid head posture, and cannot be used while resting in bed or during transit.
AirWhisper's Breakthrough: Instead of demanding fine finger control on a glass display, AirWhisper leverages the Apple Watch's built-in accelerometer, gyroscope, and orientation sensors to create an intuitive, hands-free communication lifeline.

2. Mandatory Apple Watch Setup (Do This First!)

Before launching AirWhisper on the Apple Watch, you must configure three critical watchOS settings. These ensure the watch does not prematurely sleep, lock itself due to tremors, or require tiny touch taps.

1

Turn ON AssistiveTouch

Why: Allows patients with tremors or partial paralysis to control the watch without touching the screen using simple hand clenches or finger pinches.

How: Watch Settings → Accessibility → AssistiveTouch → Toggle ON. Then tap Hand Gestures → Toggle ON.

2

Turn OFF Wrist Detection

Why: When patients rest their arms on hospital beds, wheelchair trays, or tables, tremors cause standard sensors to lose skin contact. watchOS locks the watch with a passcode prompt and terminates sensor streams. Turning this OFF keeps the watch perpetually active.

How: Watch Settings (or iPhone Watch App) → Passcode → Toggle Wrist Detection OFF.

3

Set Wake Duration to 70s

Why: By default, Apple Watch dims and sleeps after only 15 seconds. Patients with tremors or slowness of movement need adequate time to draw strokes and review letters without the screen turning off.

How: Watch Settings → Display & Brightness → Wake Duration → Select Wake for 70 Seconds.

3. Core Features & How Each Works

Feature 1: Quick Speak (Instant Vocal Phrases)

A high-contrast list of priority phrases designed for instant medical and personal communication without spelling letter-by-letter.

  • Pinned "HELP": The emergency distress button is permanently anchored at the very top of the list.
  • Adaptive Recency Ordering: Whenever a phrase is used (e.g. "I'M IN PAIN" or "WATER"), it automatically jumps to the top of the list for quick access next time.
  • Capacity & Auto-Pruning: Holds up to 10 active phrases. When the list is full and a new phrase is saved, the least-used phrase is automatically replaced.

How to Use: Select the phrase → The Action screen appears → Tap Speak Aloud (plays through watch speaker) or Send To (sends emergency SMS to caregiver).

Feature 2: Surface Tap Detection (Hands-Free Table Navigation)

Engineered for Severe Motor Impairment: For patients who cannot reliably perform finger pinches, clenches, or touchscreen taps.

The Concept: The patient rests their wrist or hand naturally on any rigid or semi-rigid surface—a hospital bedrail, bedside tray table, wheelchair armrest, or even their own leg. Tapping a finger on that surface sends micro-vibrations through the wrist to the Apple Watch accelerometer.

Navigation Mechanics:

  • Single Tap → Advance Focus: Advances the green highlight cursor to the next Quick Phrase or action item. Voice Guidance speaks the item name aloud immediately so the patient doesn't even have to look at the screen.
  • Double Tap → Select & Confirm: Two quick successive taps confirm the highlighted phrase and open the send screen.

One-Time Guided Calibration:

  1. Enable Tap Detector in the iPhone companion app. The watch automatically displays the Calibration Screen.
  2. Phase 1 (Single Taps): Press Record → tap the surface once → press Stop. Repeat 3 times. The watch learns your unique downward force peak (Z ≤ -0.5g) and rebound peak (Z ≥ +0.2g).
  3. Phase 2 (Double Taps): Perform 3 double taps. The watch learns your natural double-tap timing rhythm (~0.4s ± 0.25s).
  4. Once calibrated, the watch displays a green checkmark and saves your personalized tremor profile.

Feature 3: Air Write (Character-by-Character Recognition)

Mandatory Rule: The Apple Watch MUST be worn on the LEFT wrist. The Core ML motion model was trained exclusively on left-arm kinetic kinematics.

Detailed Step-by-Step Recognition Flow:

  1. Start: Tap the blue Start button (or clench via AssistiveTouch). Voice Guidance announces "Start Recording".
  2. Draw: Trace a single uppercase letter in the air using broad, fluid arm movements from the elbow and shoulder.
  3. Stop: Tap the red Stop button (or clench). Voice Guidance announces "Stop Recording".
  4. Sensor Capture: Watch records 6-axis IMU data at 100 Hz (3-axis gyroscope + 3-axis world acceleration).
  5. 11-Stage Preprocessing: The system removes latency flatlines, despikes high-frequency tremor jitter, calculates kinetic energy profiles, and normalizes the stroke into a standard 384 × 15 tensor.
  6. Neural Classification: The on-device IMUClassifier neural net predicts the letter. A specialized D vs. P classifier head differentiates between letters with identical starting downstrokes.
  7. Confidence Thresholding (≥ 50%): If prediction confidence is ≥ 0.50, the letter is appended to the message box and spoken aloud (*"A!"*). If confidence is below 50% (distorted by an involuntary spasm), it is safely dropped without cluttering the message.
  8. Corrections & Done: Tap Delete (←) to remove an errant character. When the word is complete, tap Done.

Feature 4: Orient Write (Wrist-Tilt Virtual Canvas)

For Patients with Limited Arm Elevation: If a patient cannot lift their arm into the air, Orient Write allows them to write using only subtle wrist tilts while resting comfortably.

  • Roll (Left/Right tilt): Controls horizontal cursor movement (X-axis).
  • Pitch (Up/Down tilt): Controls vertical cursor movement (Y-axis).
  • Dead-Zone Tremor Damping (0.05 rad): Filters out resting tremor twitches so the cursor remains steady.
  • Pen Down / Pen Up: Tap Start → cursor turns green → tilt wrist to draw → tap Stop → handwriting recognition evaluates the drawn glyph and adds the letter to your text.

Feature 5: Sending Messages & The Action Screen

Whenever a phrase is picked or custom text is finished, the Action Required screen presents 4 clear options:

  1. Speak Aloud: Speaks the full sentence through the Apple Watch speaker at full volume for immediate in-room communication with doctors or family.
  2. Send To (Emergency SMS): Opens your synced contacts list (My People). Tapping a contact immediately dispatches the text message via the cloud SMS gateway (Render + Twilio backend).
  3. Save as Phrase: Saves your custom sentence directly into Quick Speak for instant future one-tap access.
  4. Cancel: Returns back to the writing canvas to add more words.

5. iPhone Companion App Setup

  • Watch Settings: Adjust motion sensitivity slider (0-100%), toggle Voice Guidance, and turn on Tap Detector. Syncs automatically to watch via Bluetooth.
  • Manage Quick Phrases: Add custom phrases important to your daily routine (e.g., "Please adjust my pillow", "Water please"). Synced over Bluetooth and backed up to iCloud.
  • My People (Emergency Contacts): Add emergency caregiver phone numbers. When you tap "Send To" on the watch, messages are instantly dispatched to these numbers via SMS.
  • Message History: Audit log showing all messages and distress alerts sent from the watch with dates, times, and recipient names.

6. Troubleshooting & Best Practices

Symptom Probable Cause Immediate Solution
Watch screen sleeps while drawing Wake duration is set to default (15 seconds). Watch Settings → Display & Brightness → Set Wake Duration to 70 Seconds.
Passcode screen appears during arm movement Wrist Detection is ON; tremors caused sensor to lose skin contact. Watch Settings → Passcode → Turn Wrist Detection OFF.
Letters consistently misrecognized Watch worn on the right arm. Move watch to LEFT wrist (model trained specifically on left-arm dynamics).
Surface Tap does not detect single taps Tapping too softly or calibrated on a different surface. Tap Recalibrate and record 3 firmer taps on your bedside surface.
SMS fails to send iPhone disconnected or missing international dial prefix. Ensure iPhone is connected to Wi-Fi/Cellular and contact phone numbers include + country code.

7. Verbatim "How to Use" Steps From the App

AirWrite — How to Use

What is AirWrite?
AirWrite lets you compose messages by drawing letters in the air using broad arm movements—no typing or screen touch required.

  1. Wear on your LEFT hand: The IMU model is trained specifically on left-hand movements. Wearing the watch on your right hand will result in incorrect predictions.
  2. Draw the letter in the air: Trace the letter shape with your arm in one smooth motion. Draw exactly as you would if the air were your writing canvas.
  3. Confidence threshold for accepted letters: The on-device Core ML model scores each prediction. Only letters meeting the confidence threshold are added and spoken aloud. If the model is unsure, the result is silently dropped needing you to draw the letter again.

Letter Reference Carousel: Browse every letter (A–Z) to see the exact wrist motion used to train the model.

OrientWrite — How to Use

How It Works:
OrientWrite allows you to compose messages using wrist movements, including roll (left/right tilt) and pitch (up/down tilt). These wrist motions control the cursor on a virtual canvas, enabling you to draw letters directly on the watch face. As you move your wrist, the cursor follows your gestures, allowing you to write messages intuitively without touching the screen.

  1. Open OrientWrite on Your Watch: From the writing modes, tap OrientWrite. A virtual writing canvas will appear, and the watch will begin tracking your wrist movements.
  2. Keep Your Wrist Steady Before Writing: A small cursor indicates the current writing position. Hold your wrist still for a moment to allow the cursor to stabilize before you begin writing.
  3. Move the Cursor Using Wrist Tilts: Tilt your wrist left or right (roll) to move the cursor horizontally. Tilt it up or down (pitch) to move the cursor vertically. Small unintentional movements are filtered out to help keep the cursor steady and improve writing accuracy.
  4. Tap Start and Write a Letter: Tap the Start button to begin recording your input. Use wrist tilts to guide the cursor and trace a letter on the virtual canvas. OrientWrite tracks the cursor's path as you write. When you finish, tap Stop. The cursor turns green to confirm that the stroke has been successfully recorded.
  5. Wait for Letter Recognition: Once recording stops, the captured stroke data is processed by the handwriting recognition system. The system analyzes the shape drawn on the canvas, identifies the corresponding letter, and adds it to your message. The recognized letter is also spoken aloud for confirmation. If recognition is unsuccessful, try again.
  6. Correct Mistakes Using the Backspace Button: If an incorrect letter is recognized or added by mistake, tap the Backspace (←) button to remove the most recently entered character. This allows you to make corrections without restarting your message.

Surface Tap — How to Use

What is Surface Tap?
Some patients were unable to consistently perform gestures like pinch and clench. Surface Tap lets them communicate by tapping any nearby surface - a bed rail, table, or their own leg. The Apple Watch detects the vibration and helps them navigate through Quick Phrases hands-free.

  1. Turn on Tap Detector in the iPhone app: Open AirWhisper on iPhone → Home screen → Watch Settings → toggle "Tap Detector" ON. The setting syncs to the Watch over Bluetooth.
  2. Complete the one-time calibration on the Watch: The first time Tap Detector is enabled, the Watch opens a calibration screen. You'll tap a surface 3 times for single taps, then 3 times with a quick double-tap. The Watch learns your specific tap force and timing from the accelerometer data and saves it.
  3. Rest your wrist on any surface: Place your wrist or hand lightly on a bed, table, chair arm, or any flat surface. No need to move your arm - the vibration from your fingertip travels through your wrist to the Watch.
  4. Single tap → move to the next phrase: One light tap advances through your Quick Phrase list and speaks the highlighted phrase aloud. The Watch highlights each phrase in green so you can follow along visually.
  5. Double tap → select and send the phrase: Two quick taps in succession confirm the currently highlighted phrase. It opens the send screen so the phrase can be sent as a message.
  6. Tap Detector works only in Quick Speak: Surface Tap is active exclusively on the Quick Speak screen. Air Write and Orient Write continue to work via their own controls when the standard mode is active.

Quick Phrases — How to Use

What are Quick Phrases?
Quick Phrases are pre-saved sentences that the Apple Watch reads aloud the moment you tap them. "HELP" is always pinned at the top.

  1. Open the AirWhisper companion app: Launch AirWhisper on your iPhone and go to the Home screen.
  2. Tap "Manage Quick Phrases": Type a phrase in the text field and tap Add. The phrase appears in the list immediately.
  3. Use phrases that matter most: Add up to 10 phrases. If the list is full, the least-used phrase is automatically replaced — you'll see an alert when this happens.
  4. Phrases sync to your Watch automatically: Once added, phrases are sent to your Apple Watch over Bluetooth and backed up to iCloud — so they're there even after pairing a new Watch.
  5. On the Watch, open Quick Speak: Tap any phrase in the list to have the Watch speak it instantly. The most recently used phrase moves to the top for faster access next time.
